# PageSift 0.81.0 Release Notes: Relevance by Facet, Speed by Working with the Hardware

> **Release Version**: `v0.81.0`
> **Release Date**: 2026-10-29
> **License**: AGPL-3.0-only (Commercial licensing available)

---

## Overview

Two lines of work meet in 0.81.0.

**Focused crawls judge pages better.** Until now the precision of focused crawls was measured by a
grading script that looks at URLs and titles. Judging pages by reading their content showed that
the script was optimistic by more than 20 points: on 69 universities in five directions, about
45–50% of the pages a focused crawl fetched were useful, not 70%. The biggest weakness was page
relevance itself: it counted how many query words a page contained, so the generic words of a
query won. On a university site *department*, *faculty* and *research* are everywhere; a page of
another department that has all three scored high, while a page entirely about visas, which has
one word of an international-students query, scored low. 0.81.0 scores pages by facet, with every
statistic estimated from the crawl's own pages.

**Everything is faster.** Profiling with a fixed cache model (cachegrind) showed where the time
goes: hundreds of thousands of small allocations, tables stored cell by cell on the heap, and a
quadratic title check. These are fixed by working with the hardware (contiguous data, inline short
strings, byte-run processing, an allocator cache per thread and NUMA node), and the release
binaries are now built with whole-program LTO and profile-guided optimisation. Converter output is
byte-identical to 0.80.10.

**Behaviour changes to know about when upgrading:**

- Relevance values (`relevance` in `index.jsonl` and in crawl results) are on a new scale: lower
  in absolute terms (most useful pages are between 0.05 and 0.4). Compare pages within a crawl, not
  against thresholds taken from earlier versions.
- Focused crawls stop at different points than before; adaptive crawls on synthetic sites use about
  10% fewer pages for the same share of useful pages.

## Page relevance by facet

The new relevance model (`crates/pagesift/src/focus/facet.rs`):

- **Units.** The query words, plus every pair of adjacent query words that the site's own pages use
  as a phrase: the pair occurs side by side much more often than the two words' frequencies predict
  (binomial approximation, more than three standard deviations above the expectation). On the
  university sites this finds *computer science*, *international student*, *student housing*,
  *opening hours*, *application deadline* and *admission requirements* without any list.
- **Evidence per unit**, each between 0 and 1, averaged: the unit is in the title or the URL (the
  page is *about* it), the unit is in a heading, and its body frequency saturated by page length
  (`tf / (tf + 1 + length / mean length of the site's pages)`).
- **Weight per unit**: its inverse document frequency among the pages fetched so far, so words that
  every page has stop counting.
- **Relevance**: the weighted mean of the evidence, between 0 and 1, times the existing hub and
  furniture discounts.

### How it was chosen

1,900 pages were judged by reading their content (1,833 of them are in the recordings and were
scored) (useful / marginal / not useful; rubric and labels in
`benchmarks/history/0.81.0/`): 400 from earlier crawls and a stratified
sample of 1,500 from recordings of 61 universities × five directions (pages the focused crawls
fetched, and pages a breadth-first crawl fetched, as negatives and as positives the focused crawls
never reached). The universities were split in two by a hash of their name; the model was designed
on one half and checked on the other.

| Separating … (AUC) | old relevance | facet relevance |
|---|---:|---:|
| useful or marginal vs. not useful, held-out universities | 0.789 | 0.864 |
| squarely on the query vs. the rest, held-out universities | 0.859 | 0.915 |

Candidates that were measured and rejected: query likelihood with Dirichlet smoothing (0.766),
query-term proximity (worse), latent semantic analysis, and pseudo-relevance feedback on top of the
facet model (0.79 combined: the expansion picks up function words and drifts, e.g. visa and tuition
for a housing query on pages of an international office).

The two adaptive-stop thresholds were converted on the same judged pages so that they keep the same
share of useful pages: "irrelevant" below 0.05 (was 0.12; about 90% of useful pages are above it)
and "evidence" from 0.15 (was 0.3; about 52%).

The synthetic focused-crawl gate (`focus_gate`): budget 12 pages 83.8% → 83.6% useful,
adaptive 83.6% → 84.2% with 120 pages instead of 134.

### Also in focused crawls

- Subtrees and clusters are kept or dropped by successive elimination with 95% Wilson intervals on
  their harvest rate (no fixed thresholds); blind links are probed one at a time; once the query
  is covered and only blind links remain, the crawl stops.
- On an English start page, `english.<site>` (an English department) is no longer taken for the
  English version of the site.
- The start page is retried up to four times; a dead host is dropped after two failures instead of
  six.

## Performance

Deterministic counts with cachegrind on a fixed cache geometry (32 KB L1, 8 MB LL; estimated cycles
= instructions + 10 × L1 misses + 100 × LL misses), 0.80.10 against 0.81.0 without PGO, same
machine (`scripts/perf_counts.sh`):

| Benchmark | instructions | L1 misses | LL misses | estimated cycles |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HTML (perf gate pages) | −26.35% | −18.09% | +0.08% | **−25.99%** |
| PDF (paper, 3 runs) | −47.62% | −53.14% | +0.31% | **−47.88%** |
| CSV (4 files incl. Shift-JIS, GBK) | −63.18% | −65.46% | −72.56% | **−64.62%** |

Other measurements, same machine:

- 394 real web pages, main-content extraction: instructions −34.8% from the title check and scans
  alone; multi-threaded CPU time a further 3–9% lower with the allocator cache.
- xlsx with 20,000 rows × 12 columns: instructions −31%.
- PDF conversion of the benchmark paper: 42.6 → 36.6 ms with the allocator cache.
- Local crawl gate (20 sites × 60 pages): 1,087 → 1,253 pages/s, CPU time −13%, peak RSS 31 → 33 MB.
- PGO on top of all this (Linux x86-64, one process per file, `convert` of 115 real web pages): user
  CPU time about 9% lower; the binary is 11% smaller. Training data: `scripts/pgo_train.sh`.

What changed:

- **Allocator cache** (`pagesift-sys`, used only by the CLI binary; the Python and Node bindings keep
  the host's allocator). Allocations up to 512 bytes and 16-byte alignment come from per-thread
  LIFO free lists in 16 size classes; empty lists are refilled in batches of 32 from global pools,
  whose lock only moves one pointer. The pools are sharded by NUMA node (looked up with
  `sched_getcpu` and the node map in sysfs) so threads on one socket never contend with the other;
  each pool has its own 128-byte line. A thread keeps at most its share of the L2 cache (L2 size ÷
  logical CPUs sharing it, from sysfs); per-class limits start at one batch and grow only when the
  thread keeps asking. These follow the lessons of the 192-vCPU, four-NUMA-node runs of 0.70.
- **PDF**: glyph text is an inline 22-byte string (no allocation for nearly every glyph); line
  building reuses buffers.
- **HTML**: the `<title>` comparison builds its signature once per page and stops as soon as a match
  is impossible (it used to be quadratic on unclosed headings); attribute, author-list and date scans
  skip ahead bytewise.
- **CSV**: one contiguous buffer for the whole table; fields, escaping and cell cleanup copy byte
  runs found with memchr instead of pushing character by character.
- **xlsx**: number formats compiled once per format code; attributes unescaped only when their name
  matches.
- **Build**: fat LTO, one codegen unit (CLI binary 42 MB → 25 MB). Release binaries for Linux x86-64,
  Linux arm64 and macOS arm64 are built with profile-guided optimisation, trained on generated pages
  and the repository's document fixtures; if any PGO step fails the release falls back to the plain
  build.

Output stays byte-identical to 0.80.10: 394 web pages, 271 PDFs and 377 Office and CSV files compared
in full.
