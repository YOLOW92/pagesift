# PageSift 0.80.2 Release Notes: Focused Crawling That Finds the Right Pages

> **Release Version**: `v0.80.2`
> **Release Date**: 2026-10-28
> **License**: AGPL-3.0-only (Commercial licensing available)

---

## Overview

0.80.2 is about `crawl --query`: the focused, best-first crawl that is supposed to spend a small
page budget on the pages that answer a question. A tester ran it over 159 university websites
with the query *international students admission tuition scholarship visa*, 12 pages per site, and
got 25.6% useful pages (3.5% specific to international students); on 47 of the sites it brought
back nothing useful at all. The link scoring, the scheduling and the stopping rule are rewritten
in this release. Two PDF follow-ups from the 0.80.1 review and two flaky macOS tests are fixed too.

- **Focused crawl rewritten.** Every link is scored from its anchor text (navigation links
  included), the words in its URL and the line it sits on; the query terms are weighted by how
  rare they are on the site; news, press, events, about, legal and dated paths are demoted; the
  queue alternates between sections instead of draining one; a weak link is not fetched while a
  strong page is still in flight; subdomains of the same registrable domain are in scope by
  default; error pages are not content; the adaptive stop only takes evidence from relevant pages.
- **New: `attachments`.** PDFs and Office documents linked from relevant pages are fetched with
  their own budget and go through the existing PDF/Office pipeline.
- **New: crawl trace.** Each result says how it was reached: `metadata.pagesift.crawl` with
  `depth`, `parent`, `score` and `attachmentOf`; `index.jsonl` carries the same columns.
- **PDF titles under bookmarks (0.80.1 S-4 follow-up).** A PDF whose bookmarks cover the sections
  but not the title no longer comes back with an empty `metadata.title`.
- **Truncated linearized PDFs (0.80.1 D-2 follow-up).** A file cut before its page tree root is
  no longer rejected as "no pages": the page objects that survive are converted, in order, with
  the `pages_missing` warning.
- **macOS CI.** Two timing-sensitive tests no longer fail on a slow runner.

**How this was verified.** The container this was developed in cannot reach any university
website (every request is refused at the network edge), and no saved copies of those sites exist.
All crawling claims below are therefore **covered by local test cases only**: eight synthetic
campus sites served in-process (`crates/pagesift/examples/focus_gate/`), each built around one
failure pattern seen in the tester's report, two synthetic sites from other domains with their own
queries (a SaaS developer portal, *api authentication rate limit webhook*; a city services site,
*passport renewal fee appointment*) that guard against tuning the rules to universities, plus the
integration tests in `crates/pagesift/tests/focused.rs` and `focused_site.rs`. The tester's 159-site numbers are theirs,
measured on 0.80.1; nothing in this release has been run against those sites. Expect the real-site
precision to differ from the synthetic gate.

## Focused crawl

### What went wrong on 0.80.1

The 0.80.1 focused crawl scored a link by the query words in its anchor text and in the Markdown
line around it, but only for links that appear in the extracted main content. Navigation links,
which is where a university's "International students" entry lives, were scored from the URL
alone; most of them scored 0, and ties were broken by discovery order, so the crawl degenerated
into breadth-first over the home page's first links: news, events, about. A news story that
mentions "students" scored as well as the admissions page. Services on `international.example.edu`
were out of scope unless `allowSubdomains` was set. The adaptive stop counted "students" in news
stories as evidence and declared coverage reached after a dozen useless pages, or saw nothing new
and gave up. Error pages (a 404 template with the site's navigation) counted as pages.

On the synthetic gate (8 sites, 12 pages each, `--adaptive`), 0.80.1 brought back 7 useful pages
out of 96 (7.3%; 1.0% specific to international students; median 1 per site; 3 sites with
nothing). The per-site table is in `benchmarks/history/0.80.2/focus-0.80.1.txt`.

### What changed

All of this is in `crates/pagesift/src/focus/` (one module per concern) and the crawl runner.

- **Anchor text for every link.** The crawler now receives the anchor text of every link on the
  page (`Document.link_text`, internal), so navigation links are scored like body links. A link's
  raw score is `2.0 × anchor coverage + 1.5 × URL coverage + 1.0 × context-line coverage + 0.5 ×
  parent page relevance`, plus a bonus when the anchor hits two or more query terms, times
  `0.9^depth`. The URL coverage looks at the host labels (`international` in
  `international.example.edu`), the path and the query string.
- **Term weights by document frequency.** After three pages, each query term is weighted by how
  rare it is among the pages crawled so far (`ln((N+1)/(df+0.5)) / ln(N+1)`, clamped to
  0.15–1.0). "students" appears on every page of a university site and stops counting; "visa"
  and "scholarship" do the work. Coverage is the weighted share of query terms hit.
- **Furniture, dated leaves and hubs.** URLs and short anchors naming news, press, blog,
  changelog, events, tags, search, sitemaps, about, history, leadership, governance, awards,
  privacy, accessibility, terms, contact, login, careers, giving, alumni, exhibitions, museums,
  people and `/.well-known/` are multiplied by 0.2; a word the query itself asks for is never
  furniture (`/docs/api/` is a content link for an *api* query). Paths with a year or a
  `/2026/05/` date are dated leaves (news releases, posts, minutes) and are multiplied by 0.1:
  however on-topic, they lead nowhere. A page whose text outside link labels is outnumbered by its
  links (a hub or an article list, even one whose titles cover the whole query) has its relevance
  halved. A page that links more than 8 times into one section (a news list) has each of those
  links discounted by `sqrt(8 / n)`.
- **Sections before leaves.** A link to a top-level section (`/docs/`, `/services/`,
  `/admissions/`, a subdomain root) with a short anchor gets a floor score worth a furniture link
  whose anchor names half the query, so "Docs" and "Services" are explored before dated news that
  happens to mention the query, but after any link whose anchor actually names a query term.
- **Scheduling.** The queue pops across sections: when one section (host plus first path
  segment) already holds a third of the budget, the best link from another section is taken
  instead if it scores at least half as much. A slot is left empty rather than filled with a link
  scoring under 60% of the best page in flight, so the links of a strong page can be queued before
  the weak ones are fetched.
- **English portals.** For an ASCII query on a non-English site, the "English" / "EN" entry and a
  `/en/` directory get a floor score, since the English content lives behind them and the entry
  itself contains no query terms.
- **Subdomains.** A query crawl follows subdomains of the same registrable domain by default
  (`www.example.edu` → `international.example.edu`); `allowSubdomains: false` turns it off. Crawls
  without a query keep the old default (same host only).
- **Error pages.** In a query crawl, a response with status 400 or above is reported through the
  error callback (`HTTP 404: not a content page`), not as a page, and its links are not followed.
- **Adaptive stop.** Pages below relevance 0.3 contribute no evidence. Before anything relevant
  has been found they do not count at all (portals and news clusters sit in front of the content
  as a matter of course); once relevant pages have been collected, a run of irrelevant pages as
  long as `patience` ends the crawl as `saturated`, unless the best link still queued scores
  higher than every page in that run: the best candidates have not been tried yet, which is what
  happens when a section page comes back and its children are only now queued.

On the synthetic gate (8 campuses, `--adaptive`) 0.80.2 brings back 57 useful pages out of 69
fetched (82.6%; 63.8% specific to international students; median 8 per site; no site with
nothing), using 69 of the 96-page budget because the adaptive stop ends each site early. On the
two other-domain sites, added after the university rules were written, it brings back 9 target
pages and 2 adjacent ones out of 19. The first version of the rules brought back none on either
site: `api` sat in the furniture lexicon, section links without query words lost to dated posts,
and a home page listing twenty post titles counted as relevant. The four rules that fixed that
(query words are never furniture, dated leaves ×0.1, the section floor, hubs measured by prose
outside link labels, and the saturation override) are general and left the campus results
unchanged. Over all 10 sites: 68 of 88 (77.3%); with `--attachments` 75 of 94 (79.8%); without
`--adaptive` 98 of 120 (81.7%). The gate and its thresholds (`MIN_PRECISION` 0.75 over all sites,
no zero-score site) run from `scripts/ci.sh`. These are synthetic numbers; see the verification
note above. The measurements are in `benchmarks/history/0.80.2/`.

### `attachments`

Tuition tables, policies and application guides are often PDFs hanging off the relevant page, and
often outside the crawl's path scope (`/files/…`). With `"attachments": true` (CLI
`--attachments`), a PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint or EPUB link on a page whose relevance is at
least 0.3, on the same registrable domain, is queued separately, ordered by the same link score,
with its own budget (`attachmentLimit`, CLI `--attachment-limit`, default `limit`). Attachments are
fetched when no page is ready to schedule and after the adaptive stop, so they do not displace
pages. They go through the existing PDF/Office conversion; the result's trace names the page that
linked them. `attachments` needs `query`.

### Crawl trace

Every crawled document now carries `metadata.pagesift.crawl`:

| Field | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| `depth` | links from the seed (seed is 0) |
| `parent` | the page that linked it (absent for the seed and sitemap URLs) |
| `score` | the link score at queueing time (absent for the seed) |
| `attachmentOf` | for attachments, the page that linked the document |

`pagesift crawl --out` writes the same fields, with `relevance` and the HTTP `status`, to
`index.jsonl`, so a crawl that went wrong can be read back: which link brought the news cluster
in, and with what score.

### Not changed

A seed page whose links only appear after JavaScript runs is not re-rendered by the focused crawl
in this release; the engine's usual `auto` fallback applies to each page as before. The tester's
suggestion to restrict the crawl to a hand-written list of path patterns was not adopted: the
scoring above is meant to make such lists unnecessary, and the gate's `trap` and `gov` sites show
the same effect without them.

## PDF titles under bookmarks (0.80.1 S-4 follow-up)

**Before.** 0.80.1 inferred the title from the first `#` heading of the first page. When a PDF has
bookmarks or a structure tree, pagesift uses the writer's headings and nothing else; arXiv papers
typically have bookmarks for the sections (`1 Introduction`, …) but not for the title, so the title
was an ordinary paragraph, no `#` heading existed above the sections, and `metadata.title` stayed
empty. The tester confirmed this on the real arXiv paper after 0.80.1; reproduced here with a
generated paper whose bookmarks name the sections only.

**Now.** In that mode, the first page's largest short paragraph (at most 3 lines and 200
characters, at least 1.25× the body size and 2 pt above it, larger than any writer-tagged heading
on the page, not itself tagged) is promoted to the level-1 heading, and the writer's headings move
down one level when they started at level 1 (`# Attention Is All You Need`, then
`## 1 Introduction`). `inferred_title` and `metadata.title` (`titleSource: "inferred"`) follow.
Files whose writer tagged a heading as large as the candidate are unchanged.

Local test cases: `crates/pagesift-pdf/src/structure/title.rs` (the candidate rule and its
refusals) and `crates/pagesift-pdf/tests/extract.rs` (a bookmarked paper without `/Title`).

## Truncated linearized PDFs (0.80.1 D-2 follow-up)

**Before.** The tester cut the linearized arXiv paper to 55% and got a clean error, "document has
no pages", instead of the recovered pages and the `pages_missing` warning that 0.80.1 added. In a
linearized file the first page's objects come first and the `/Pages` root often sits in the half
that was cut away, so the page-tree walk finds the catalog and then nothing.

**Now.** When the page tree yields no page, the file layer collects every `/Type /Page` object the
file still contains, in object-number order, with the page named by the linearization dictionary's
`/O` first; the dictionary's `/N` (or the largest `/Count` of any surviving `/Pages` node) is the
declared page count, so the warning reads, for example, `pages_missing: 4 of 8 pages could not be
recovered from the damaged file; 4 converted`, next to `pdf_repaired`. Without a linearization
dictionary or a `/Pages` node the declared count is unknown and only `pdf_repaired` is reported.
Intact files never enter this path.

Local test cases: `crates/pagesift-pdf/tests/extract.rs` (a hand-written 5-page file with its
root removed and the tail cut, with and without a linearization dictionary). Also run on a
generated 8-page linearized paper cut at 55% with its object stream blanked: 4 pages, both
warnings. The real arXiv file was not available in the development container.

## macOS CI

- `tests/multihost.rs` asserted that subdomain pages finish within 600 ms of wall clock; a slow
  runner took 852 ms. It now asserts that they finish before the tenth of the thirty serialised
  slow pages, which is what the test is about.
- `full_page_scan_triggers_lazy_loading` (pagesift-cdp) once found no lazily loaded content on a
  runner that otherwise passed the same test. The full-page scan now waits a whole `scroll_delay`
  before its last bottom-of-page check, since a scroll handler's fetch can trail the scroll by more
  than the 100 ms quiet window on a loaded machine. The failure was not reproduced locally
  (browser tests do not run as root), so this is a tolerance change, not a verified fix.

## Upgrading

- A query crawl follows subdomains by default. Pass `allowSubdomains: false` (CLI: it follows the
  new default; there is no flag to turn it off yet, set it in the JSON request) to keep the 0.80.1
  behaviour.
- A query crawl reports error pages through the error callback instead of as pages.
- The adaptive stop ends sooner when the pages after the relevant ones are irrelevant.
- Bookmarked PDFs without `/Title`: sections that were `#` are now `##` under the promoted title.
- New request fields: `attachments`, `attachmentLimit`; `allowSubdomains` is now tri-state
  (unset means "follow for query crawls"). New response field `metadata.pagesift.crawl`. The MCP
  `pagesift_crawl` tool accepts `query`, `adaptive`, `attachments` and `attachmentLimit`.
- Library: `pagesift_api::types::CrawlTrace`; `Document.link_text` (not serialised);
  `pagesift_content::site_key`.
