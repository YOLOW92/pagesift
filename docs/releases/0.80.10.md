# PageSift 0.80.10 Release Notes: An Adaptive Page Limit

> **Release Version**: `v0.80.10`
> **Release Date**: 2026-10-28
> **License**: AGPL-3.0-only (Commercial licensing available)

---

## Overview

A focused crawl (`crawl --query`) used to need a page limit, and the limit decided how much was
found: on 69 universities in five directions with `--limit 12`, 181 of 307 crawls
stopped because the limit was used up, not because the site had nothing more, and the outside
tester's University of Manchester library crawl filled all 30 pages of `--limit 30`. In 0.80.10
the crawl decides how many pages it needs: with `--adaptive` and no `--limit`, it keeps going
while pages add new relevant content and stops when they stop doing so, or when nothing relevant
can be found; the limit is only a cap. The fourteenth round of outside testing also found
discovery problems (subtrees the crawl never reached, foreign-language main sites taking the
budget); those are fixed as general rules.

Three extraction problems from the AiSeek report are fixed as well: a press-release template
that gave a 500-character teaser instead of the body, news sidebars kept as body text, and
relative links in uploaded HTML that pointed to `file:///`.

**Behaviour changes to know about when upgrading:**

- `crawl --adaptive` without `--limit` is capped at 500 pages instead of 100. With `--limit`
  nothing changes about the cap.
- An adaptive crawl no longer stops the moment the query is covered; it stops when the pages
  that follow add nothing new. Adaptive crawls therefore deliver more pages.
- A new stop reason, `no_relevant_pages`.

---

## The adaptive page limit

`crawl --query … --adaptive` no longer needs `--limit`. Without it the crawl is capped at 500
pages (the HTTP API's `limit` already was a cap); the number of pages comes from three rules:

- **Covered and nothing new.** Reaching the coverage threshold no longer stops the crawl at
  once: the Manchester library run reached it while one branch library after another was still
  coming in. The crawl goes on while pages add new relevant content and stops after
  `ceil(patience / 2)` pages in a row that add nothing, unless the best queued link still carries
  a sign of the query. Coverage needs evidence from at least two pages; one overview page can
  mention every query word often enough (a Tsinghua housing crawl used to stop on its 2nd page).
- **Nothing new** (`saturated`): unchanged, `patience` pages in a row without new relevant
  content, unless better links are still queued.
- **Nothing to find** (`no_relevant_pages`, new): while nothing relevant has been found,
  `2 × patience` pages in a row reached only through links without any sign of the query (a
  score no higher than the section prior) stop the crawl. Such sites used to run to the limit:
  at `--limit 100`, 17 of the 25 crawls that used the whole limit had no useful page at all. The
  factor 2 was chosen by replaying 0.80.9's `--limit 100` results: it saves about a quarter of
  the pages and loses under 5% of the useful ones, all on sites where the crawl wandered for dozens
  of pages before stumbling on content.

Measured on the same 69 universities and five directions as 0.80.9 (international students,
graduate admission, housing, computer science, library; robots.txt ignored so every version sees
the same pages), 297 crawls that every run reached, graded by URL and title with 0.80.9's
rules (U: squarely on the query, M: related):

| Run | Pages | Median per site | U pages | U share | U+M share | Sites with no U |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.80.9, `--limit 12` | 3,223 | 12 | 645 | 20.0% | 48.8% | 107 |
| 0.80.9, `--limit 100` | 10,787 | 26 | 1,856 | 17.2% | 48.0% | 61 |
| 0.80.10, adaptive, no limit | 16,351 | 48 | 4,208 | 25.7% | 66.1% | 52 |

The adaptive crawl reads more pages where there is more to read (the largest crawl took 321
pages, none reached the cap) and a larger share of what it reads is useful. Stop reasons:
`coverage_reached` 192, `saturated` 72, `no_relevant_pages` 32, `exhausted` 1. Raw results and scripts: [`benchmarks/history/0.80.10/`](../../benchmarks/history/0.80.10/).

## Discovery fixes

- **Foreign-language main sites.** For an English query, section links get a minimum score so
  that portals are explored before news. On sites whose main language is not English this sent
  the crawl into the German (TUM) or Norwegian (Oslo) main site, whose sections the query cannot
  read. Once the site's English content is known (the start page is English, or the English home
  page was found), links on pages in other languages no longer get that minimum; queued ones are
  re-scored when the English home page is found. `/english/research/` now counts as a
  first-level section of the English site, like `/en/research/`.
- **Services under "about".** A short section named after a query word under an about or news
  section (`/about/housing/`, `/news/international-students/`) is content. The University of
  Washington's home page links "Student housing" to `/about/housing/`; it was demoted as an about
  page and the housing crawl found no housing page in 12. Long article slugs under `/news/` stay
  news.
- The Manchester library case (the library subsite first reached around the 14th page) is
  covered by the adaptive limit: the crawl no longer ends before it gets there.

## Extraction fixes (AiSeek report)

- **Teaser instead of the body.** Some press-release templates put the headline and a
  truncated summary in their own `<article>` and the body outside it; the article that owns the
  headline was taken as the body, so about 500 characters ending in "..." came out instead of
  3,300 to 11,600. When that article is short and its text reappears in a longer block elsewhere
  on the page, the longer block is the body and the headline is kept.
- **Sidebars kept.** In two-column layouts without an article marker the body region stopped at
  the container of both columns, so the other column's story list came out as body text. The
  region (from either the container rules or the Readability-style scoring) is now narrowed
  from the heading that matches `<title>` to the smallest container that still holds most of
  the body text. Story cards (a link next to a short summary ending in an ellipsis) do not count
  as body text. When the page's only `<h1>` is a section name and an `<h2>` matches `<title>`,
  the `<h2>` is the headline; taking the section name used to widen the region to include the
  sidebar.
- **Relative links in uploads.** `POST /v2/parse` takes an optional `url` field, the address the
  uploaded HTML came from. Relative links resolve against it (a `<base href>` in the page still
  wins), and it becomes `metadata.sourceURL`. It is never fetched. Uploaded HTML now also gets
  the completeness warnings (`partial_content` and the others) that fetched pages get.

On 394 saved pages (random web pages, the Firecrawl set's snapshots and the report's samples),
outputs changed only on the samples and on four other pages, where a logo, a newsletter box and
a "recently viewed" panel are now left out. Truth recall on the Firecrawl set is unchanged.
