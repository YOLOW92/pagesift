# PageSift 0.80.0 Release Notes: PDFs That Keep Their Structure, Results That Admit What They Missed

> **Release Version**: `v0.80.0`
> **Release Date**: 2026-10-27
> **License**: AGPL-3.0-only (Commercial licensing available)

---

## Overview

0.80 is about correctness and trust, with no loss of speed or memory:

- **Own PDF file layer.** lopdf is gone. pagesift now reads xref tables and streams, object
  streams, filters and the standard security handler itself, and repairs broken files the way
  pdf.js and MuPDF do. It opens 15 more files of the pdf.js test corpus and is faster and smaller.
- **PDF headings with real levels.** Headings come from what the writer declared — the structure
  tree and bookmarks — and are inferred from numbering, size and weight only when neither exists.
- **Headers, footers and figures.** `/Artifact` marks, running heads with page numbers and vector
  figures are recognised, so page furniture and chart labels no longer pollute the text.
- **Upload a file: `POST /v2/parse`.** Firecrawl-compatible multipart upload for PDF and HTML.
- **Warnings when content may be incomplete.** `partial_content` and `replies_omitted` flag
  pages where the article mode picked the wrong region or dropped forum replies.
- **Fixes** for batch scrapes blocked by slow pages, MCP audit records showing `client: unknown`,
  and `pagesift doctor` writing to the audit log.

Every number below says which direction is better and where it comes from. The PDF set is the 13
PDFs from the AiSeek research report (central-bank and government reports, arXiv papers, Japanese
filings, Wikipedia exports) plus olmOCR-bench (1,403 pages) and the pdf.js test corpus (1,010
files). Measured on an i7-10700 (8 cores / 16 threads) while other builds were running; timings
are the best of several interleaved rounds.

## PDF headings

Writers already say what their headings are; 0.80 reads that first (ISO 32000-1 §12.3.3 and
§14.7–14.8):

1. **Structure tree.** `H1`–`H6`, `H` (level from the enclosing `Sect`s) and `Title`, mapped
   through `/RoleMap`, attached to text lines by marked-content IDs. Lines of one element form one
   heading.
2. **Bookmarks.** Each outline entry is matched to a paragraph on its target page (and the pages
   next to it) using the destination's y position. A heading glued to the following body text is
   split off.
3. **Inference** when neither is usable: numbering styles and depth (`3.2.1`, `（１）`, `第3章`,
   `【…】`, …), font-size tiers, bold fonts by foundry naming rules; lists, captions, folios,
   years and wrapped sentences are excluded.

A structure tree is used when it tags at least two lines; bookmarks when at least half of the
entries match. Bookmarks that are file names (seen in two Japanese FSA reports) are ignored.

13 AiSeek PDFs, headings against hand-labelled truth (higher is better, except empty headings):

| | Recall | Precision | Level agreement | Empty headings |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.73.1 | 0.595 | 0.400 | 0.819 | 3 |
| 0.80.0 | **0.945** | **0.872** | **0.956** | **0** |
| 0.80.0, inference only | 0.910 | 0.598 | 0.860 | 0 |

Headings glued to body text (a missed heading that starts a body paragraph; lower is better):
15 → 1.

Held-out set, collected after the rules were fixed (16 random arXiv papers from 8 fields, a Bank
of England report, two FSA reports, one bilingual Hong Kong annual report; truth = the files' own
bookmarks): the full pipeline scores recall 0.831, precision 0.895, level agreement 0.993, no empty
headings, no crashes. Inference alone (bookmarks ignored) reaches recall 0.714, below the 0.75 we
set beforehand; precision 0.549 and level agreement 0.888 met their targets.

## Headers, footers and figures

- Text marked `/Artifact` is dropped (§14.8.2.2) unless the mark contradicts the page: an
  Artifact with a marked-content ID, text clearly larger than the body (a slide title marked as
  a footer), or a page where most text is marked.
- Running heads and feet that carry the page number (`4 Monetary Policy Report`,
  `Monetary Policy 41`) are recognised when the number minus the page index is constant on at
  least three pages — odd/even and per-chapter heads included.
- Body line spacing is measured on body lines only. Dense chart labels had pulled it down, and
  the last body lines of several Federal Reserve report pages were removed as footers.
- Vector figures (lines, curves, bars, tick rows) are detected; text inside and labels next to
  a figure become one raw block that is not parsed as a table or a heading. A plot grid (at least
  four crossing lines each way, mostly empty cells, points or lines drawn inside) counts as a
  figure, so scatter plots whose points are far apart are found too.
- Not tables: a ruled grid with text in only one column (a text box; a table has content in at
  least two columns, as Camelot's lattice mode also requires), and any candidate that overlaps a
  detected figure (none of the 143 real tables does).

## Own PDF file layer

`crates/pagesift-pdf/src/file/` replaces lopdf: lazy object loading with a cache, stream data
left in the input buffer, xref tables and streams with `/Prev` and `/XRefStm`, startxref offsets
corrected within ±64 bytes, a full-file repair scan, object streams, Flate/LZW/ASCII85/ASCIIHex/
RunLength/Brotli with PNG/TIFF predictors, and RC4, AESV2 and AESV3 (R5/R6) with crypt filters.
pagesift-pdf no longer depends on lopdf (it stays as a dev-dependency that builds test PDFs).

- olmOCR-bench + AiSeek, 1,416 files: 1,414 identical outputs, 2 fixed (CFF encoding supplement).
- pdf.js test corpus, 1,010 files: 15 files that failed now open (page counts match `pdfinfo`),
  9 read more text (for example `crypt_filters`: 5 lines instead of 2). Two files
  (`issue19484_1/2`) still fail, as with lopdf and poppler.
- 4,848 randomly truncated or corrupted PDFs: no panic, none over one second.

## `POST /v2/parse`

Upload a PDF or HTML file as `multipart/form-data` (`file`, optional `options` JSON, up to
50 MB); the response matches `/v2/scrape`, so `queryFilter`, chunking, summaries and JSON
extraction work on uploads. `parsers` supports `mode`, `maxPages` and `pages`; `ocr`, `blocks`
and `pageMarkers` are rejected explicitly. Uploading the 13 AiSeek PDFs gives Markdown identical
to scraping the same files. See [the parse API](../parse-api.md).

## Warnings when content may be incomplete

- `partial_content`: the output covers under 30% of the page's non-link body text (outside
  navigation, link blocks and comments) and at least 500 characters are missing.
- `replies_omitted`: the selected content sits in a comment region whose text is more than twice
  the output (forum threads where only the first post survived).

## Fixes

- **Batch scrapes waited for slow pages.** The per-host hot-connection controller held its
  slots for requests still waiting for a first byte, so a fast page behind slow ones on the same
  host waited for them. Requests past the tail threshold (2 s) now give up their slot (still
  within `max_connections_per_host`). Local test, 3 cores, 5 slow pages of 30 s ahead of a fast
  one (lower is better): the fast result arrives after 2.0 s instead of 30.0 s.
- **MCP audit records said `client: unknown`** for clients on the older protocol, which send
  `clientInfo` only in `initialize`; sessions now remember it.
- **`pagesift doctor` wrote an audit record** and a snapshot of its self-test; it no longer does.

## HTML parsing and hardening

- **html5lib tree-construction tests** (now maintained by WPT) are wired into the test suite; every
  remaining difference that changes extracted text is listed with its reason. Pass rate on the
  1,739 document cases 77.7% → 89.3% (higher is better); all 1,973 cases, now including fragment
  parsing and scripting-off, 90.5%; differences that affect body text 143 → 6 (lower is better).
  Fixed: `</script>` inside `<!--<script>` no longer ends the script (old ad code leaked into
  text), unclosed `<select>` no longer swallows the rest of the page, `--!>` ends a comment,
  `<template>` and table/foreign-content scoping rules; elements record their namespace (SVG and
  MathML integration points follow the spec); frameset documents, NUL characters and
  `<plaintext>` are built as the spec says. pagesift-html gains `Html::parse_with` with
  `ParseOptions` for fragment parsing (a context element) and parsing with scripting off.
- **A stable-toolchain mutation fuzzer** (`pagesift-fuzz`, `scripts/fuzz.sh`, not published) for
  HTML, Markdown, charset detection and PDF found inputs that hung or exploded; all are fixed
  (time on the found input, lower is better):

| Case | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Many unmatched `[` in text (720 KB) | 19.5 s | 24 ms |
| Hundreds of nested lists (320 KB) | 23.5 s, 46 MB output | 0.15 s |
| `<picture>` with many `<img>` (1.6 MB) | 52 s | 0.19 s |
| Deeply nested `<header>` (1.8 MB) | 4 s | 0.57 s |
| `:nth-child` over 76,000 siblings | 6 s | 3.6 ms |
| PDF object stream with thousands of ids at one offset (440 KB) | killed at > 3 GB | 81 ms, 73 MB |
| PDF text at coordinates near 1e9 (150 KB) | 21 s | 0.69 s |
| PDF `stream` without `endstream` (630 KB) | 20 s | 1.6 ms |

Extracted text is unchanged on AEB (181 pages) and the Firecrawl set (597 pages) and differs on
2 of 2,008 WCXB pages; F1 (higher is better): AEB 0.958 → 0.958, WCXB 0.8533 → 0.8534, Firecrawl
content score 0.682 → 0.682.
